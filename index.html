Phonological Reason: Why ‘-ül-’ not ‘-il-’ or ‘-in-’?

Turkish vowel harmony operates under the strict Dörtlü Vokal Uyumu (4-Way Vowel Harmony). The verbal stem vowel in göm- is an Ö (a rounded, front open vowel).

  • check_circle Stems with Ö and Ü require the high front rounded allomorph -ül-.
  • check_circle Because the root ends in a consonant (m) rather than L or a vowel, the passive voice takes -il / -ül / -ul / -ıl instead of -in.
lightbulb

Theological Semantic Shift (Divine Passive)

In Hellenistic biblical hermeneutics, the passive voice often denotes the Passivum Divinum (God as the unstated transcendent agent).

The believer does not bury oneself (gömün-, reflexive), nor do believers bury each other (gömüş-, reciprocal). The passive marker -ül- explicitly mirrors the believer being acted upon through baptismal solidarity with Christ.

Morphological Confirmation & Cross-Reference

The affix -ül- is an allomorph of the passive marker (-il / -ıl / -ul / -ül), required here by the front-rounded root vowel ö in göm-. Compare this with vaftiz edilerek earlier in the same verse, where et- takes -il- because e is an unrounded front vowel.
